# iRacing Coach: mastery

For Joshua and for every agent that touches the project. Built 2026-10-09 from `requests-catalog.md` (his 264 asks), `requests-catalog-AUDIT-codex.md` (what the catalog got wrong or missed; its items are cited as A-rows), `built-ledger.md` (what exists) and `GAP-LEDGER.md` (the 29 entries G1-G29 that close the distance). Dates are 2026. Where a specification or an agent summary and his own words differ, his words are used.

## 1. What Coach is for, in his words

"The goal of the app is to be my crew chief and engineering companion. To help me ascend to an elite racer. To understand how to be faster on the track, to understand how hard I'm being on the tires, to understand what pit strategy has the highest chance of victory, everything in this nature." (08-17)

"the overall purpose of the app is to be able to review my performance of course, but ultimately to improve ... the tire modeling/AI coaching is lackluster ... the hardest and most useful things remain frustratingly lacking" (08-15). "I need a companion. Not a fancy app that doesn't help." (09-22). The case that defines it: "there was a recent Bristol race (non fixed) where my car was dominant early. Then, in the last section of the race ... I just completely lost pace and got dominated ... that is the kind of thing I want to open the app and have it help me figure out what the hell happened." And the form the answer must take: "show me data/trends/proof or whatever of the conclusions and tell me what I needed to do differently." (09-22)

The north star (09-23): **"I would rather an app that has 5 features that win me more races than an app that has 1000 features, graphics, and charts that don't help me win any more races."** Its companion: "as capability increases complexity cannot - at least not from the user's interface and experience ... it needs to be simple, powerful, and.... beautiful." He never listed the five; the number is a standard, not a quota, and the design principles say so.

Scope, in his words over time: "personal simulator PC only. proceed with this expectation" (08-15, A4); "the entire idea is that I hope for it to be a personal project ... I want them [Garage61] to be able to see what I produce and integrate any of the features/inspirations into their own website. that would not bother me" (08-18, A17); "it is meant to more or less be an app that maybe other people use and they won't have a lab" (10-04). It runs now as a mini-PC service with a browser UI he and his guests reach over the tailnet, with no login.

## 2. The five things that win races

Assembled from his own sentences (08-17, 08-18, 09-22, 10-04, 10-09). He did not number them; each is mapped to the gap entries that deliver it.

1. **Find the time on the track.** "To understand how to be faster on the track"; "show me telemetry differences between the fastest segments and the lowest segments. but note that the segment before can impact the speed/situation going into the section"; "it does not tell me how far into a green run that is ... I don't get any information about how much my car is slipping/sliding." Delivered by G6 (the comparison picture), G4 (sectors), G5 (slip, gaps, rubber per lap), G7 (corner playbook and targets), G23 (reference laps, Garage61 or the bot's). Today: Sections shows the three fastest and three slowest clean laps per detected section with four channels and real racing lines; the time-gap and slip channels, the approach and the reference are missing.
2. **Know what I am doing to the tires.** "to understand how hard I'm being on the tires"; "all these things combine to a predictable amount of tire wear... it is all known physics in the real world, none of it is guesswork"; "your laps around the track and data gathering is simply a reverse-engineering of the math." Delivered by G8 (tire stress on the run cards), G18 (the tire and physics model and its scorecard), G5. Today: tire temperatures, pressures and remaining tread are served in `runs.json` and read by no screen; the model is "collecting" and unidentified (physics audit, 10-09).
3. **Pick the strategy that wins.** "to understand what pit strategy has the highest chance of victory"; "how many laps should I do between pit stops? how aggressively should I drive in the beginning?"; "the standard should be one lap of reserve"; "the worst thing the race planning could do is outright lie about something, but I really feel like we should have enough information to make educated guesses." Delivered by G11 (the pre-race brief), G10 (the race card's Start / Long run / Strategy tiles), G3 (penalties and repairs on the stop), G22 (the field at the cursor, "was that stop worth it?"). Today: the plan decision is computed for every recorded race (`strategy.forecast.race_plan_decision`) and shown nowhere; there is no Plan page.
4. **Bring the right car.** "what setup should I be using? what are the strengths and weaknesses of the setup?"; "our 'setup shop' where we compare, analyze, and maybe even build setups"; "show the parameters that changed and how it impacted pace & tire life"; "a damn good tuning setup ... with very little iteration." Delivered by G15 (performance per setup and version), G16 (tuning proven on his recordings, with history), G17 (baseline from physics and the track), G14 (qualifying review), G19 (`.sto` read, write, apply; parked to the utility). Today: the library fills itself, versions diff, compare works, Find a baseline is donor-based; no outcome figure anywhere.
5. **A crew chief on the wall, and the loop that proves it.** "to be my crew chief and engineering companion"; the Driver / Crew Chief live modes with "the track map with all the cars on it ... when each car's last pit stop was and which tires they took"; "an engineer/crew chief/driver brief going into the race"; and after the race, "tell me what I needed to do differently", checked on the next recording. Delivered by G20 (Crew Chief mode), G21 (live line and inputs), G12 (trials saved and checked), G13 (test day review), G25 (the driver's record), G24 (Ask, behind a key). Today: Live shows the player's 45 channels and 15 cues; nothing of the field; no trial survives a session.

## 3. Where the app is today (0bf443a, 2026-10-09)

1. Rust collector on the sim PC (1.7 MB, 0.26 % of a core idle after the fix, overlay and tray), Rust server with the Python worker in one container on the mini PC, raw recordings on the NAS, TypeScript SPA over the LAN and the tailnet, no login.
2. Destinations: Sessions, Setups, Live, plus the four-tab investigation (Race, Runs, Sections, Traces). No Plan, Driver, Details, Replay, Tests or Ask page.
3. Sessions: one card per event, R/Q/P lines, finish against the grid, DNF, best and pace from green laps, stops, burn per lap, long run, filters in the heading, phase toggles.
4. Race: lap dots, position line from lap 0, caution band, pit pictograms, incident tags, one observed-trend line per run with its slope, track temp / usage / fuel toggles. Runs: overlaid on green exposure, cards that toggle. Sections: three fastest and three slowest clean laps per detected section, real racing lines, Tires and Traffic, four channels, Open in Traces. Traces: 75 signals at 640 bins, continuous cursor, layouts, spotlight, rotated maps.
5. Setups: self-filling library (recordings, `.htm` exports, garage capture), car sections, versions with parameter diffs, compare, Find a baseline, Tune this setup with a deterministic recommendation.
6. Live: 32 tiles over 45 channels at 60 Hz with a governor, direct-manipulation editor, per-device layouts, the rig overlay pushed from the server; the 15 text cues unchanged.
7. The backend computes and the browser never shows: technical insights, strategy planning, pace attribution (time lost and tire energy per corner), groove evolution, race replay, setup telemetry, Garage61 references, race grades, field pace, tire learning, comparisons and hypotheses, the plan decision, penalty and repair seconds, tire temperatures and pressures per run, the race card.
8. Unwired worker tools: plan, test-day review, driving trials, Garage61 sync, strategy history, Ask.
9. Six fix rounds since 10-04 addressed 97 numbered complaints; round 6 (#85-#97) is in source and unreleased; nothing has been accepted by him for any scope.
10. Unproven on hardware: sim impact on a moving car (2.34 % against the 1.5 % budget before the fix, not re-measured), a real phone, tablet or rig screen, collector autostart, a real NAS outage, and the tuning workflows on a real recording.

## 4. The roadmap we should be working towards

Each phase names what it lets him do in a race week (practice midweek, race at the weekend) and ends on its check, not on its code.

**Phase 0, this week: trust the list.** G1 (release round 6, reanalyze, fix the stale docs), G2 (every race listed, the missing Charlotte race diagnosed, the real result after a wreck-out), G27 (the quiet status glyph), G26 part 1 (G4 re-run on a moving car). Race week: every race he drove is on the list within two minutes with its finish, and what he fixed on 10-07 is in front of him.

**Phase 1: the investigation answers "what happened".** G6 (time gap, slip, approach, run age on Sections), G4 (sectors), G5 (slip, gaps, rubber per lap), G3 (penalties and repairs), G8 (tire stress on the run cards), G7 (where the time is lost and what it costs the tires), G9 (stop and fuel figures on the run lines). Race week: Sunday he opens the race, finds the sector where the fade began, reads entry speed, slip, tire laps and traffic behind it, and sees what the stop cost. Check: the Bristol question answered without a sentence, on his own recording.

**Phase 2: the brief and the loop.** G10 (Start / Long run / Strategy tiles), G11 (Plan for any installed car and layout), G12 (save the one change, verdict on the next recording), G13 (test day review), G14 (qualifying), G15 (performance per setup and version), G16 (tuning history and a real-recording acceptance). Race week: Tuesday he opens Plan for Saturday's track (stops, reserve, which setup and its outcome history), saves a test for Thursday's practice, and the verdict appears on the race card afterwards. Check: the planned stop window against the stop he made; a trial verdict that is never "unchanged".

**Phase 3: the crew chief.** G26 complete (moving-car gate, real devices, autostart, the acceptance record format of A8), then G20 (Crew Chief mode and the read-only page), G21 (live line and inputs, if he confirms it), G22 (the field at the cursor). Race week: during the race a watcher sees every car, its last stop and its gap; after it, the recording shows the same field at the cursor. Check: the stream's cost inside the G4 budget on a moving car; the live field agreeing with the recording.

**Phase 4: knowledge.** G23 (reference laps from Garage61 and the Lab in the rail), G17 (baseline from physics and the track, once the Codex baseline brief lands), G18 (the model's scorecard on the run cards and the ranked experiments in the utility), G24 (Ask behind a key, answers as evidence rows only), G25 (the driver's record). Race week: his lap against a reference in the sector where he loses most; a baseline sheet with predicted temperatures and pressures, then the measured ones. Check: the prediction error shrinking race over race.

**Every phase:** G28, the look-and-motion loop. Each release gets his look on the laptop, the tablet, the rig and his phone; his complaints become the next round; the colour budget, the one type scale and the centred-control recipe hold throughout.

**Parking lot, until he says otherwise:** G19 (`.sto` decode and write; the harvest when the sim is free), G29 (small restores and the open decisions #32, #42, #84).

**The stop rule.** Before any screen, chart, menu or button: name the racing decision it improves, what he can do differently, and how a later recording shows whether it helped (`companion-design-principles.md`). An entry that cannot name its check does not ship; a phase ends when its check has run on a real recording of his, not when the code lands. No destination beyond Sessions, Setups, Live, Plan and Driver (the two he asked for by name); nothing in the product that only his lab, the bot or the agents use. When a required input is absent the surface is absent: "I don't want things in the app that will never be available, or almost never be available." And the critique and the coding are separate: "no coding. just giving feedback. I'll tell you when to code" (09-21, A20).
