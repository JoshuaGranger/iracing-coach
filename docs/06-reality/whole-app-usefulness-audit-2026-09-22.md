# Whole-app usefulness audit — September 22, 2026

## Scope and standard

This audit reviewed why each visible action exists, what its handler actually does, whether the result helps the driver make a decision, and where the journey stops. The baseline was development source `7e9a356dec0fe659b04ff93b81c6c24d36f8dbd3`, before this round's companion and note-persistence changes.

The source inventory contains **257 control declarations and gesture families in 25 product files**, including generated row/button families, disclosures, chart/layout interactions, onboarding, the native popout and system tray. Repeated controls in different states are counted separately; dynamic instances are grouped by their action. This is a source inventory, **not a claim that 257 native controls or every possible state passed testing**.

The [full control-by-control audit](whole-app-control-audit-2026-09-22.md), [declaration appendix](whole-app-control-inventory-2026-09-22.md) and [machine-readable source inventory](whole-app-control-inventory-2026-09-22.json) are tracked with this document. ## Additional defect found during the control audit

Tuning notes were debounced for 350/450 ms, then discarded by turn/phase/race changes or component disposal before persistence. The correction captures the text and original turn/phase/draft at typing time, flushes before dependent actions and on disposal, and binds writes before waiting for the state mutation gate. Delayed edits merge into the original stored draft without replacing another selected race. A replaced map draft is rejected.

Changed files: [feedback editor](../../companion-app/src/iRacingCoach.UI/ProgressiveTuningFeedbackEditor.razor), [tuning page](../../companion-app/src/iRacingCoach.UI/TuningPage.razor), and the tuning note/feedback methods in [CompanionState](../../companion-app/src/iRacingCoach.Coordinator/CompanionState.cs). Five behavioral [regressions](../../companion-app/tests/iRacingCoach.Tests/TuningNotePersistenceTests.cs) cover immediate phase/turn change, dispose after callback change, general-note race change/dispose, two queued writes followed by race change, and stale-map rejection. The focused affected suite passed **56/56**. Native focus and navigation behavior still needs its own pass.

## Native finding: contradictory Bristol fuel story

The DESKTOP-SIM **0.17.7 isolated baseline** opened September 20's 3:09 PM Bristol race and its lap-102 stop. The overview reported a **+12.2 green-lap post-stop margin**, while the same stop detail showed **55.4 laps of range versus 56.0 laps remaining**. The native evidence is `QA-USEFULNESS-BASELINE-20260922.md` and `QA-USEFULNESS-0177-pit-fuel-contradiction.png` in the QA share. This was an observed defect, not an inference from source alone.

Inspection of the redacted real analysis established the cause: **55.42 was the pre-service range**. The next run started with 34.196 L, yielding **68.19 green laps** at the measured burn rate; 56 scheduled laps remained, so the projected margin was **12.19 laps**. The mapper had put `fuel_laps_remaining_at_end` into a field labeled after-stop range. No caution adjustment or reserve difference explains that substitution.

The **0.18 development source** reads `post_stop_all_green_range_laps` and keeps missing/null post-stop measurements absent rather than substituting pre-stop fuel. The UI names the projection's basis: post-stop green range before reserve, and scheduled laps left at the next run start. The generic SAFE status now reads Projected surplus. This does not claim every future lap will use the same fuel. The forecast's configured reserve is a separate planning quantity; the post-stop range/margin do not subtract it. Backend range text now uses the actual reserve rather than saying two laps unconditionally. Pit-cycle advice also follows whether positions were gained, unchanged or lost; a +2 cycle no longer receives an instruction assuming a loss. Producer regression covers negative/zero/positive net positions crossed with missing, one-lap and three-lap reserve cases.

Five new [presentation regressions](../../companion-app/tests/iRacingCoach.Tests/PitFuelPresentationTests.cs) cover the real numeric contradiction, absent/null post-stop range, a valid zero range and overview selection. The affected suite passed **28/28**. **Native candidate 2 verified the corrected 68.2 range, 56 remaining and +12.2 margin**, including appropriate +2-position pit advice (`QA-0180-pit-range-fixed.png`). Missing/null cases remain automated rather than claimed native passes.

The 1280×720 browser review also exposed overcrowding in the thirteen-metric Racecraft overview. Its overview now selects six purpose-specific slots: overall position change, long-run pace change, fastest/median pace, variation and incidents. All available measurements remain in the drilldown, including controls and race phases. This change reduces overlap without dropping the detailed evidence or changing the fixed two-by-two overview.

## Narrative rule

“Review the evidence before assigning cause” is not coaching if the app leaves the review to the driver. A useful statement identifies the measured change and the comparison: which laps, how much pace, where in the corner, changed inputs, tire age, laps since caution, traffic and conditions. When a cause is uncertain, propose a test that distinguishes the plausible explanations.

Remove routine claims such as “local monitoring stays local,” “your data is safe,” “evidence-backed” and “what to carry forward” where they do not change a decision. Keep material limitations in plain language beside the affected result. “Track usage increased, but this recording cannot isolate its effect from changed driving inputs” can be useful; a generic unavailable card repeated across the page is not.
