Section bars compare Run 1 laps 35, 36, 37 with Run 2 laps 130, 131, 132 by group median. Traces compare the fastest and slowest actual passes among those six laps. Sections A–E are analysis regions, not Bristol turn numbers. Section E combines the former sections 5–7 as requested; its timing and selected laps are recomputed across the full 77.5–86% interval. The other turn boundaries still need verification.

Green laps are accumulated sampled green-time equivalents since the run began, including running between cautions—not consecutive green laps or measured tire wear. The exact value beside each lap changes with the selected pair. Fuel differs: 15.21 versus 5.67 gallons across the groups; both report 78°F. Similar exposure does not mean identical tires or grip.

Body slip is chassis sideslip derived by the backend from recorded longitudinal and lateral velocity. Each stored distance bin retains its signed peak. It describes how the car moves relative to its heading, not individual tire slip, wheelspin, available grip or proof of a slide. Matching the faster pass's input curves is not itself a driving target.

Time gap starts at zero at the displayed approach origin. A gap present at entry and a gap added inside are observations, not proof of cause. Run lines show a centered five-observation median, using three or four observations at sequence edges. They include traffic-affected laps and describe recorded results, not clean-air potential or tire degradation. Hollow dots are excluded from fair comparison; outer rings mark the six compared laps. Trend segments span their supported observation windows. Sequences with fewer than three observations remain dots only. No lap-zero time is invented; missing laps and exposure discontinuities break the line. Screened linear fits remain confined to their supported range in the method figures below.
